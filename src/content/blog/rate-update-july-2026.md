## Rates Edge Lower This Week

We saw a welcome shift in the mortgage market this week. The 30-year fixed rate dropped to 6.43% as of July 2nd, down from 6.49% the previous week. While a 0.06% decrease might sound modest, it's a move in the right direction after months of elevated rates. The 15-year fixed rate currently sits at 5.79% for buyers considering that option.

## What This Actually Means for Your Monthly Payment

Let's talk real numbers. On a $500,000 home purchase with 20% down (a $400,000 loan), here's what this week's rate drop means:

- **At 6.49%**: Your monthly principal and interest payment would be approximately $2,528
- **At 6.43%**: That same payment drops to about $2,512

That's a savings of roughly $16 per month, or nearly $200 annually. Over the life of a 30-year loan, you're looking at close to $5,800 in savings. Not life-changing for most buyers, but definitely meaningful - and every basis point counts when you're making one of the biggest purchases of your life.

For context, every 0.25% change in rate translates to about $60-65 per month on a $400,000 loan. So if rates continue trending downward, the savings accelerate quickly.

## The Historical Context You Need

Here's the reality: 6.43% is still elevated compared to the rock-bottom rates we saw from 2020-2021 (remember when 3% felt expensive?). But historically speaking, we're actually closer to normal than many buyers realize. Throughout the 1990s and 2000s, rates in the 6-7% range were standard, and the housing market functioned just fine.

The 2020-2021 period was the anomaly, not our current environment. Those ultra-low rates were a response to unprecedented economic conditions, and they helped create the intense seller's market and rapid appreciation we experienced. Today's rates are simply a return to longer-term norms.

## Tactical Advice for Right Now

**For Buyers:** Don't wait for rates to hit some magical number before you act. If you've found the right home in the right neighborhood at a price that works for your budget, these current rates shouldn't stop you. Remember, you can always refinance later if rates drop significantly, but you can't go back and buy that perfect house once someone else does. We're also seeing less competition in our market compared to a year ago, which means better negotiating position for you.

**For Sellers:** Pricing remains critical. Buyers at 6.43% are more payment-sensitive than buyers were at 3.5%, which means they're calculating backwards from monthly payment to purchase price. Work with your agent to price competitively from day one. Overpriced homes are sitting longer in our current Twin Cities market, while well-priced properties are still moving within a reasonable timeframe.
