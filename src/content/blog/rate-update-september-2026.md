## Rates Edge Higher This Week

Mortgage rates continued their upward drift this week, with the 30-year fixed rate reaching 7.03% as of September 24th. That's up from 6.95% the previous week - a modest 0.08% increase that nonetheless represents the continuation of a trend we've been watching closely. The 15-year fixed rate now sits at 6.42%.

While eight basis points might not sound dramatic, we know that even small rate movements matter when you're financing a home purchase in our market.

## What This Means for Your Monthly Payment

Let's put some real numbers to this. On a $500,000 mortgage (roughly what you'd need for a median-priced home in many Twin Cities suburbs), the difference between last week's 6.95% rate and this week's 7.03% translates to about $25 more per month in principal and interest. Over the life of a 30-year loan, that's approximately $9,000 in additional interest.

For a $400,000 loan, you're looking at about $20 more monthly. For $600,000, it's around $30. These aren't make-or-break numbers for most buyers, but they do add up - and more importantly, they signal which direction the wind is blowing.

## Historical Context: Where Do We Stand?

Here's the perspective that matters: yes, 7.03% is significantly higher than the extraordinary sub-3% rates of 2020-2021. Those rates were historically anomalous, driven by pandemic-era monetary policy. But if we zoom out further, current rates aren't apocalyptically high.

In the 1990s, rates frequently sat in the 7-8% range during a healthy housing market. In the early 2000s, they bounced between 5-7%. The difference today is that home prices are also elevated, creating a double affordability challenge that the rate alone doesn't fully capture.

The reality is we're in a higher-rate environment than we've seen in 15 years, and we need to plan accordingly rather than waiting for a dramatic reversal that may not materialize.

## Tactical Advice for Right Now

**For Buyers:** This isn't the time to wait for perfect conditions. If you're financially ready and find the right home, current rates - while higher than we'd all prefer - shouldn't paralyze you. Remember, you can always refinance if rates drop, but you can't recapture a home that was perfect for you. We're also seeing less competition than in previous years, which gives you more negotiating leverage and time to make thoughtful decisions.

Consider exploring different loan products beyond the standard 30-year fixed. Some buyers are finding value in adjustable-rate mortgages or buying down their rate with points if they have extra cash available.

**For Sellers:** Higher rates haven't killed demand in the Twin Cities, but they have made buyers more selective. Pricing needs to be sharp and based on truly comparable recent sales. Homes that show well and are priced correctly are still moving - often with multiple offers. Homes that aren't are sitting longer than they would have two years ago.
