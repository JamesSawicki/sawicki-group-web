After a tight winter, April brought more homes to market across the Twin Cities. According to Minnesota Realtors®' April 2026 report, new listings in the metro were up 8.9% from April 2025, and the number of homes for sale was up 7.2%.

That's meaningful. Here's what it means for buyers in the market right now.

## What the numbers show

April 2026 in the Twin Cities metro, compared with April 2025:

- **New listings:** up 8.9%
- **Signed purchase agreements:** up 6.9%
- **Homes for sale:** up 7.2%
- **Median sales price:** $392,000, down 2.0%, the first year-over-year decline since 2023
- **Days on market:** 57 on average

Buyers and sellers were both more active, and prices softened a little. The report calls it a return to a more balanced market. Even so, inventory is still well below what a balanced market needs.

Activity wasn't the same everywhere. Closed sales fell 6.1% in Minneapolis but rose 6.8% in Saint Paul. By price, sales under $300,000 dipped 2.6%, and sales over $1 million fell 13.9%.

## What it means for your strategy

**If you're buying in a competitive neighborhood or price range:** More listings help at the margins, but well-priced, well-presented homes still draw strong interest. Get your financing in order, know your number, and be ready to move when the right home appears.

**If you're seeing homes sit:** Longer days on market give you room to negotiate. Ask about closing cost help, keep your inspection contingency, and don't waive protections just because buyers had to a few years ago.

**If you've been waiting for prices to fall sharply:** A 2% dip in the median is normalization, not a crash. Waiting means waiting through more rate uncertainty, not necessarily less competition.

## The rate question

More inventory is good news. Rates are the counterweight. On a 30-year fixed mortgage, every $100,000 borrowed costs about $615 a month in principal and interest at 6.25%, or about $650 at 6.75%. That math is real, and it shapes what you can afford.

The buyers doing well right now plan around today's rates, not a hoped-for future rate, and focus on finding the right house at the right price.

## Bottom line

More inventory is good for buyers. Use the extra selection to be more deliberate. Don't let a calmer market make you complacent about preparation: financing, inspection strategy, and offer structure still matter.
