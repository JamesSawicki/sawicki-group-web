## Where Rates Stand This Week

According to Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey, the average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage came in at **7.32%** as of October 22, 2026. That's down from **7.40%** the week before, a decrease of **0.08 percentage points**. The 15-year fixed rate sits at **6.68%**, continuing to offer a meaningful discount for buyers who can manage the higher monthly payment that comes with a shorter loan term.

It's a modest move, but in a market where every fraction of a percent affects buyer budgets, it's worth paying attention to.

## What This Actually Means for Your Monthly Payment

Let's put real numbers behind this. On a **$350,000** loan with a 30-year fixed term:

- At **7.40%** (last week): principal and interest would run about **$2,424** per month
- At **7.32%** (this week): principal and interest comes down to about **$2,404** per month

That's roughly **$20 a month**, or about **$240 a year**, saved purely from this week's rate movement. Not life-changing on its own, but it illustrates something important: small rate shifts add up, and they can affect how much home you qualify for or how comfortable a payment feels.

If you're considering the 15-year fixed at 6.68% instead, that same $350,000 loan would run closer to **$3,069** a month. The payment is higher, but you'd build equity faster and pay significantly less interest over the life of the loan. Whether that trade-off makes sense depends on your cash flow and your long-term plans, which is a conversation worth having before you lock in anything.

## Reading Into the Week-Over-Week Dip

One week of rate movement doesn't establish a trend, and we're not going to pretend it does. What we can say is that rates moved in a favorable direction for buyers this week. If you've been sitting on the sidelines waiting for a lower rate, this is a small signal that conditions can shift, and being pre-approved and ready to act matters more than trying to perfectly time a bottom.

## Practical Advice for Buyers

**Get a rate lock conversation going early.** If you're actively house hunting in Minneapolis or Saint Paul, talk to your lender about how rate locks work and what happens if rates move before you close.

**Run the numbers on both loan terms.** Don't default to a 30-year fixed just because it's common. If your budget can handle a higher payment, the 15-year rate at 6.68% could save you a substantial amount in interest over time.

**Don't wait for a "perfect" rate.** Rates fluctuate week to week. Focus on what payment you're comfortable with today rather than trying to predict where rates go next.

## Practical Advice for Sellers

**Understand how rate sensitivity affects your buyer pool.** Even small rate improvements, like this week's, can bring buyers back into a price range they thought was out of reach. Pricing your home accurately for today's rate environment matters more than comparing to what the market looked like months ago.

**Be ready to talk financing with buyers.** Buyers working with knowledgeable agents and lenders are more likely to move forward confidently, which benefits you as a seller too.
